# Ankle Sprain Rehabilitation

## Overview

An ankle sprain is an injury involving the ligaments that support the ankle joint. It commonly occurs when the ankle rolls, twists, or turns beyond its normal movement.

Symptoms may include pain, swelling, tenderness, bruising, reduced range of motion, and difficulty putting weight on the affected foot.

## Early Recovery

During the early stage of recovery, rehabilitation should focus on protecting the injured area while maintaining comfortable movement.

General rehabilitation approaches may include relative rest, gradual movement, and monitoring symptoms.

Activities that significantly increase pain or swelling should be reduced.

## Mobility Exercises

Gentle ankle mobility exercises can help maintain or gradually restore range of motion.

Examples include:

- Ankle pumps
- Gentle ankle circles
- Ankle alphabet movements
- Controlled dorsiflexion and plantarflexion

Exercises should be performed within a comfortable range and should not significantly increase pain.

## Strengthening

As symptoms improve, rehabilitation may gradually include strengthening exercises.

Examples include:

- Seated heel raises
- Standing heel raises
- Resistance-band ankle movements
- Controlled balance exercises

Progression should be gradual and based on tolerance.

## Balance Training

Balance exercises can help improve ankle stability after an injury.

Examples include:

- Single-leg standing
- Supported balance exercises
- Progressively more challenging balance activities

Use appropriate support when necessary to reduce the risk of falling.

## Activities to Avoid

During periods of significant pain or swelling, activities that place excessive stress on the ankle may need to be reduced.

Examples include:

- Running
- Jumping
- Rapid changes of direction
- High-impact sports

Return to these activities should be gradual.

## Warning Signs

Professional medical evaluation may be appropriate when symptoms are severe, worsening, or not improving.

Seek prompt medical attention for situations such as:

- Inability to bear weight
- Severe or increasing swelling
- Significant deformity
- Severe pain
- Numbness or loss of sensation
- Symptoms that do not improve as expected

## General Guidance

Rehabilitation should be individualized based on the person's injury, symptoms, medical history, and recovery stage.

The information in this document is general educational information and does not replace evaluation or treatment from a qualified healthcare professional.
